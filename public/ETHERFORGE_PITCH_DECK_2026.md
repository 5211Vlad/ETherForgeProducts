# EtherForge

**Practical local-first software today. Evidence-governed AI infrastructure for high-trust workflows tomorrow.**

## 1. Problem
Operational AI and software workflows often fail in expensive but ordinary places: long-running workflows resend history to stay coherent, project handoffs lose source context, changed data is hard to audit before import, and AI outputs can obscure evidence, uncertainty, and disagreement.

## 2. Solution
EtherForge builds bounded, local-first tools around those failure points instead of forcing every problem into one giant platform.

Current public product/evaluation lanes include:
- **RowGlass** — local CSV change inspector.
- **Thread Junk Remover** — local transcript signal extractor.
- **Release Sentinel** — release-safety scanner.
- **Handoff Forge** — source-aware handoff packet builder.
- **CSV Rescue Desk** — CSV cleanup and normalization.
- **SOPHIA 2.6** — private local Windows companion for practitioner workflows.
- **METRON 2.0** — context-efficiency and AI FinOps research product, with external benchmarking as the next gate.

## 3. Technology
**MAGI** explores evidence-governed multi-perspective analysis:
1. Independent analytic perspectives
2. Evidence and provenance ledger
3. Adversarial review
4. Bounded synthesis that preserves uncertainty and dissent
5. Human authority for consequential decisions

The architecture is designed to be model-agnostic and local-first where useful, with continuity across sessions/providers and explicit evidence boundaries.

## 4. Market
Initial users and partners include AI/LLMOps teams, operations teams, agencies and developer teams, research and engineering groups, and organizations running long-lived AI workflows where continuity, provenance, reliable handoffs, and cost discipline matter.

## 5. Why now
AI adoption is moving faster than the operational controls around it. As organizations use more models and longer-running workflows, continuity, provenance, auditability, context efficiency, and human control are becoming infrastructure requirements.

## 6. Business model
Near term: one-time software sales, paid pilots, evaluation engagements, and service/subscription tiers where justified.  
Growth: B2B workflow tooling, measured AI-infrastructure pilots, partnerships, and licensing where evidence supports it.
