ABOUT ETHERFORGE

Human + AI, forged into useful tools.

EtherForge is a small Alabama product studio and research forge. The public shop sells narrow software. Under it sits a deeper lab exploring what happens when continuity, provenance, evidence, and eventually machines are treated as first-class engineering problems.

THE SHORT VERSION

Make the weird useful.

EtherForge did not begin with a plan to build a giant platform. It grew from repeated friction: files that could not be trusted after a change, conversations too long to reuse cleanly, projects that lost their continuity between people and AI sessions, release folders that were one missed check away from embarrassment, and model workflows that kept paying to reread history.

The response was to build tools—first for the problem directly in front of us, then to harden the underlying methods until they could survive outside the lab.

THE JOURNEY

One brick at a time.

Constraint became a design method. Rather than wait for a perfect lab, perfect hardware, or a giant funding round, EtherForge kept turning internal problems into small external products while the research stack matured underneath.

01
The first brick

RowGlass proved the operating model.

Take a boring, concrete failure mode—“what changed in this CSV before I import it?”—and build a local tool that gives the operator a legible answer without demanding a cloud platform. That became the pattern: narrow job, visible evidence, untouched source.

02
The product pile grows

Internal utilities became customer-shaped software.

Transcript cleanup became Thread Junk Remover. Release preflight became Release Sentinel. Project continuity became Handoff Forge. Messy exports became CSV Rescue Desk. Each one pushed the same ideas—local execution, non-destructive handling, receipts, and bounded claims—into a different practical corner.

03
The lab becomes visible

METRON turned a recurring AI cost problem into a pilot.

Long-running AI systems repeatedly pay to ship history back into models. METRON emerged from the attempt to reduce that waste without sacrificing continuity or pretending a smaller prompt is automatically a better outcome. The product is built and hardened; external before/after validation is the next gate.

04
Avalon under the floorboards

The strange work gets a sandbox.

GhostOS, MAGI, continuity research, evidence councils, and future embodied systems need more freedom than a storefront can safely provide. Avalon became the conceptual lab boundary: hypotheses may be bold inside; public claims still have to pass boring evidence gates before they leave.

05
Now

Fund the lab by shipping the edge.

The current strategy is deliberate: sell useful local tools, recruit pilot customers, acquire compute/fabrication resources, and use every external interaction as feedback. The long-term work stays ambitious; the commercial work stays specific enough for somebody to buy this week.

THE CULTURE

Professional enough to trust.
Wonky enough to remember.

Forged metal, glass, cyan ether, strange project names, and a research-lab vocabulary are part of the brand because they are part of how the work is actually organized. The weirdness is allowed to be visible. The evidence boundary is what keeps it professional.
